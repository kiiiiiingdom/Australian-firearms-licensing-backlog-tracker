# Jurisdiction Tracking Plan

This file records the official-source coverage for each Australian jurisdiction. It is a source map, not a ranking.

## South Australia

Authority: South Australia Police (SAPOL)

Primary processing metric:
- Processing date for firearms licence applications.

Primary processing source:
- SAPOL — Status of my application
- https://www.police.sa.gov.au/services-and-events/firearms-and-weapons/status-of-my-application

Observed/source cadence:
- SAPOL states that the table is provided each week; practical monitoring cadence: daily capture, expecting roughly weekly changes.

Staffing/capacity sources:
- SAPOL Proactive Disclosure / FOI determinations.
- FOI Determination 26-1424 states that the Firearms Branch Registry and Adjudication sections have 17.8 substantive FTE positions; temporary/additional staffing is acknowledged but not quantified.
- The same determination states Firearms Branch does not set specific processing-time KPIs or targets because of processing complexity and variability, and warns that legacy-system statistics are intended primarily for work-querying rather than reliable performance statistics.
- Exact numeric staffing values should only be entered when the released determination/documents can be verified.

Status: active.

## Queensland

Authority: Queensland Police Service Weapons Licensing

Primary processing metrics:
- Average online processing timeframe for new licence applications.
- Average online processing timeframe for permits to acquire.

Primary processing source:
- QPS — Weapons Licensing
- https://www.police.qld.gov.au/units/weapons-licensing

Observed/source cadence:
- Page publishes a dated "Current average online application timeframes as at ..." metric. Exact update cadence is not stated; daily monitoring is sufficient.

Important methodological note:
- Queensland publishes an average timeframe, not a processing-date queue. Do not calculate backlog age from this series.

Staffing/capacity sources:
- QPS Commissioner's 100-Day Review provides official context on labour-intensive manual data entry, severe backlog, structural limitations and proposed Weapons Licensing staffing-model reform.
- It is context evidence rather than a numeric staffing count unless a specific headcount/FTE figure can be found elsewhere.

Historical/capacity evidence:
- Queensland Parliament QON 1498 (2022) states that 50 FTE positions were assigned to Weapons Licensing since June 2022.

System/workflow reform evidence:
- QPS states that work on the replacement Weapons Licensing Management System (WLMS) has commenced.
- QPS says the replacement platform is intended to improve customer experience and licensing-service efficiency and support Queensland's transition to the National Firearms Register.
- Staged releases are planned from March 2027.
- Source updated 30 September 2026; preserved at evidence/QLD/2026-09-30/wlms.txt.
- This is ICT/workflow-capacity evidence, not a processing-time metric.

Status: active.

## New South Wales

Authority: NSW Police Force Firearms Registry

Primary processing metric:
- Monthly average processing time for new licence applications.

Primary source family:
- Firearms Registry performance/data reports published as official PDFs on the NSW Police website.

Current verified source:
- Data Report March 2026 is the latest report for which exact licence-processing metrics are currently entered in the dataset.
- The official NSW Police Firearms Data page was verified on 1 October 2026 as listing a newer Firearms Registry Performance and Data Report July 2026. The July metric values are not entered until the report itself is directly captured and verified.

Observed/source cadence:
- Monthly reporting series. Daily source discovery is acceptable, but the substantive metric is monthly.

Important methodological note:
- NSW states that its average processing time includes the 28-day cooling-off period. Cross-jurisdiction comparisons must account for that definition.

Staffing/capacity target:
- NSW Police annual reports, parliamentary estimates/questions, Firearms Registry reports, audit material and official establishment documents.
- Registry-specific FTE/headcount is preferred over broader command staffing.

Historical evidence:
- As at 12 September 2022, NSW reported nil backlog for new firearm licences; most other listed Firearms Registry categories were also nil.
- In October 2021 NSW reported 10 additional staff seconded to reduce the backlog, with structural realignment intended to double internal firearms-review staff.

Status: active; historical monthly series seeded from April 2025 to March 2026, with a 2022 zero-backlog baseline.

## Western Australia

Authority: Western Australia Police Force / WA Government firearms licensing services

Current finding:
- No current recurring public firearm-licensing processing metric has yet been verified from the main public pages.
- Historical parliamentary/official material may provide point-in-time processing data and should be retained as contextual evidence, not treated as a live series.

Staffing/capacity target:
- WA Police annual reports, parliamentary estimates/questions, implementation reports associated with firearms-law reform, budget papers and official workforce material.

Historical evidence:
- September 2024: 1,354 firearm licence applications were outstanding; official correspondence referenced a potential six-week delay.
- August 2025: 1,421 broader portal-scope transactions/applications were outstanding; this count includes additions, transfers/co-licensing and new licences.
- March 2026 transition-program status: WA Parliament recorded 82,807 transitional authorities issued; 78% of individual licences transitioned or closed, 3% expired, and 19% not yet transitioned as at 6 March 2026. The final transitional authorities were due to expire on 23 August 2026.
- Preserve this as transition-program evidence rather than interpreting the 19% as a new-application backlog.

Portal-transition processing-order evidence:
- WA Police's Firearms Portal Information page, last updated 8 May 2026, states that Licensing Services are prioritising pre-registered accounts for access to the new email-login option and that new applications will be processed once those registrations are completed.
- Preserved at evidence/WA/2026-05-08/portal-prioritisation.txt.
- Treat this as workflow/prioritisation evidence only; it does not quantify processing time or backlog size.

Status: source discovery continues; historical point-in-time backlog evidence and current workflow evidence established.

## Australian Capital Territory

Authority: ACT Policing / firearms registry function

Target processing metrics:
- Official processing-time or service-standard information.
- Published licensing delay notices or periodic reports.

Staffing/capacity target:
- Registry/function-specific staffing if available; whole-of-ACT-Policing FTE is contextual only and must not be treated as firearms-licensing capacity.

Current licensing workflow/policy evidence:
- ACT Policing states that a permit to acquire cannot be issued before 28 days after the Firearms Registry receives the application. A separate ACT Policing page expresses the equivalent rule as no issue until 29 days after application.
- New firearms licence and renewal submissions require an appointment with the ACT Firearms Registry.
- From 1 July 2026, the adult firearms licence fee increased from AUD 179 to AUD 487 and the permit-to-acquire fee from AUD 20 to AUD 30.
- ACT Policing states that the new licence fee supports delivery of a new digital ACT Firearms Registry.
- Preserved at evidence/ACT/2026-10-10/licensing-policy.txt.
- The minimum-wait rule is not evidence of actual average processing time or current backlog; fee values are policy context only.

Historical workflow/capacity evidence:
- ACT Policing reported a temporary Firearms Registry processing interruption in June 2024 after a damaged water main affected the Exhibit Management Centre.
- ACT Legislative Assembly QON No. 30 (answer signed 7 March 2025) records that ACT Policing's then-current firearms data system had extremely limited search/filtering capability and some historical reporting required manual interrogation.
- The same answer states the National Firearms Register/ACT Registry uplift was expected to improve these capabilities. It records $500,000 federal funding provided to date; the Federal Funding Agreement was stated to contribute up to 75% ($18.7m) of the ACT Registry uplift cost over four years, with ACT Government contributing up to 25% ($6.2m). This is ICT/program-capacity context, not staffing FTE.

Status: source discovery required for a recurring backlog/actual-processing-time metric; current minimum-wait and workflow-policy evidence is preserved.

## Cross-jurisdiction comparison rules

1. Preserve the native metric published by each authority.
2. Never treat a statutory minimum waiting period as evidence of administrative backlog.
3. Do not compare a processing date directly with an average processing time without clearly explaining the difference.
4. Where no public metric exists, record `no_public_metric_found` rather than inventing an estimate.
5. Prefer police, justice, parliament, audit-office and other government primary sources over media or forum reports.
6. Secondary sources may be retained only as corroboration or as leads to primary evidence.
7. Record the authority's own metric date separately from the date on which the tracker retrieved it.
8. Monitoring frequency does not imply source publication frequency; daily checks are used primarily to capture dynamic pages before they overwrite historical values.
9. Keep staffing headcount, FTE, funded establishment, vacancies and assignment-specific counts separate.
10. Prefer exact firearms-unit staffing over broader licensing-division or whole-agency workforce figures for capacity analysis.
